A cosmetology state board exam is usually built in two parts: a computer-based written test of multiple-choice questions, then a separate hands-on practical exam where you perform real services on a mannequin and get graded against a checklist. That's the shape most states use, but it's not universal. California dropped the practical entirely in 2022 and now licenses on the written exam alone, and Florida splits its written portion into two separate exams instead of one.

What determines your format is which state you're licensing in and which vendor that state has contracted to deliver the exam, since the actual mechanics of exam day, computer-based versus in-person, one written test versus two, timed practical stations versus untimed, come from that vendor relationship rather than from any single national standard.

## What are the two parts, and how is each delivered?

The written part is a closed-book, computer-based, multiple-choice test given at a physical test center or, in some states, through a remotely proctored session on your own computer. You answer on screen, and in most states you see your pass or fail result before you leave.

The practical part is different in kind, not just format: you show up with your own kit and perform live services, cutting, coloring, chemical texture work, manicuring, on a mannequin head and hand while an evaluator scores each step against a written checklist. In Texas, for example, the entire practical runs 3 hours and 31 minutes across roughly ten timed sections, from pre-exam disinfection through a haircut, a permanent wave, foil highlights, and a relaxer application, ending with a mandatory disinfection section before you leave ([PSI Texas Cosmetology Operator Candidate Information Bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). Evaluators aren't allowed to speak to candidates beyond reading instructions, and every section ends the same way: stop working, raise your hand, move to the next station.

## Which states still require a hands-on practical exam?

Most do, but the list of states that have dropped it is growing. California eliminated the practical exam for every license type effective January 1, 2022, so candidates there now only sit for the written test ([California Board of Barbering and Cosmetology](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/applicants/national.shtml)). Pennsylvania's cosmetology candidate handbook states plainly that "candidates are not required to take and pass a hands-on practical examination," running a written and procedural-skills exam instead ([Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)). Texas, New York, and Ohio still require both parts.

## How many separate written exams do you actually sit for?

This is where format diverges most. Most states, including Texas and California, give you one combined written exam covering the full content outline. Florida instead splits its written portion into two entirely separate 90-minute exams: a Written Theory exam and a Written Clinical exam, each with its own 65 multiple-choice questions and its own content outline, scheduled and scored independently ([Florida DBPR Cosmetology Candidate Information Booklet](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/servop/testing/documents/cos_cib.pdf)). Pennsylvania takes a third approach, blending state-specific law questions directly into the same written exam as the general theory content rather than running a separate law test.

| State | Written exam(s) | Practical required? | Delivered by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | One combined written exam | Yes | PSI Services LLC |
| California | One combined written exam | No (eliminated 2022) | PSI Services LLC |
| Florida | Two separate written exams (Theory, Clinical) | No | Pearson VUE |
| Pennsylvania | One written exam, state law blended in | No | Pearson VUE |
| New York | One combined written exam | Yes | New York Dept. of State (self-administered) |
| Ohio | One combined written exam | Yes | Ohio Cosmetology and Barber Board (self-administered) |

## Who actually delivers the exam?

Two outside vendors handle most state cosmetology exams: PSI Services LLC, which delivers Texas's and California's exams, and Pearson VUE, which delivers Florida's and Pennsylvania's. A smaller number of states run the entire process in house. New York's written and practical exams are developed and administered directly by the state's Department of State Division of Licensing Services at its own exam sites, not through a third-party vendor ([New York Department of State, Become a Cosmetologist](https://dos.ny.gov/become-cosmetologist)). Ohio works the same way: applications and scheduling run through the state's own eLicense Ohio portal, and both the written and practical exams happen at Board-run test sites ([Ohio State Cosmetology and Barber Board, Exams](https://cos.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cos/pathway-to-licensure/exams/exams)).

The National-Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology (NIC) sits behind a lot of this without being the vendor itself. NIC develops standardized theory, practical, and written-practical exam content, but the delivery is handled by "Test Administration companies and States upon approval and authorization of NIC," meaning NIC sets the content standard while PSI, a state board, or another vendor actually runs exam day ([NIC Exam Types](https://nictesting.org/nic-exam-types/)).

## What changes about exam day depending on format?

If your state only requires a written exam, you show up with a photo ID and nothing else; there's no kit to build, no mannequin to transport, and no separate scheduling for a second test. If your state still requires a practical, exam day is a longer, more logistically involved process: you're bringing your own supplies, arriving with time to set up, and working through timed stations where you can't leave once you've checked in. States that split the written exam into two parts, like Florida, also mean two separate testing appointments and two separate score reports rather than one.

## FAQ

**Do all cosmetology state boards require a hands-on practical exam?**
No. California eliminated its practical exam for every license type in 2022, and Pennsylvania's current candidate handbook confirms candidates there are not required to pass a hands-on practical either. Most other states, including Texas, New York, and Ohio, still require both a written and a practical component.

**Is the written exam the same format everywhere?**
No. Most states give one combined written exam, but Florida splits its written portion into two separate 65-question exams, Written Theory and Written Clinical, each scheduled and scored on its own.

**Who actually runs the exam, the state or a private company?**
It depends on the state. Texas and California use PSI Services LLC; Florida and Pennsylvania use Pearson VUE; New York and Ohio administer their own exams directly through their state licensing agencies, with no outside testing vendor involved.

**How long does the practical exam actually take?**
In Texas, the full practical runs 3 hours and 31 minutes across roughly ten timed sections covering everything from disinfection setup to a haircut, a permanent wave, and chemical relaxer work, ending with a final disinfection section.

**Does NIC administer the exam directly?**
No. NIC develops the standardized theory and practical exam content used as the base for many states' exams, but actual delivery, scheduling, and scoring is handled by outside test administration companies or by the state itself, not by NIC.
