Milady is a textbook publisher, not the organization that writes or administers your state board exam. Milady is a division of Cengage Learning that publishes the cosmetology textbooks most schools teach from, plus a digital platform called CIMA with an exam-prep module. The actual written and practical exams come from PSI Services or NIC, depending on your state, and those two organizations explicitly say they weren't involved in producing Milady's study materials.

That distinction matters because "Milady practice test" gets searched by people who think Milady is the licensing body. It isn't. Knowing who actually writes your exam changes where you should be spending prep time.

## What is Milady, exactly?

Milady is a publishing division of Cengage Learning, the company behind textbooks like Milady Standard Cosmetology, the book most cosmetology programs assign. Milady's own site describes itself this way in its copyright line: "a division of Cengage Learning, Inc." ([milady.com/about](https://www.milady.com/about)). Milady is not a government agency, not a testing company, and not a licensing board. It sells textbooks, curriculum, and a digital learning platform called CIMA, with an add-on called CIMA ExamReady built specifically for exam preparation ([milady.com/cima](https://www.milady.com/cima)).

## Does Milady write the state board exam?

No. Milady's own state-by-state exam chart names the actual administrators directly, and carries this disclaimer verbatim: "NIC is a trademark registered and/or owned by the National-Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology. PSI is a trademark registered and/or owned by PSI Services LLC. These organizations and individual state licensing boards were not involved in the production of and do not endorse this product" ([milady.com/about](https://www.milady.com/about)). NIC's executive director is quoted on the same page acknowledging that "Milady's materials are regularly referenced in our exam content, ensuring alignment with current industry practices and educational benchmarks," which is a materially different claim than writing the exam. Reference material and exam ownership are two different things, and the distinction is the whole reason this guide exists.

## Who actually administers cosmetology licensing exams?

| Organization | Role |
|---|---|
| PSI Services LLC | Administers the written and practical exam in most states, including Texas via TDLR |
| NIC (National Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology) | Develops standardized exam content used by many member state boards |
| Your state licensing board (TDLR, a state board of cosmetology, etc.) | Sets the requirements, contracts the exam vendor, issues the license |
| Milady / Cengage | Publishes textbooks and a study platform; has no role in writing or grading your exam |

If your exam is state-specific, like Texas's TDLR cosmetology exam, go to that state's official candidate bulletin for the exact content breakdown and passing score rather than relying on a general Milady-branded number, since Milady's materials are national in scope and your state's weighting can differ.

## How should I actually use Milady-based study material?

Treat Milady's textbook and CIMA platform as your content source, the place you learn the material, and treat your state's official candidate bulletin as your map of what's actually tested and how it's weighted. Texas's current PSI-administered written exam, for example, weights Infection Control at 28% of the scored questions, Hair and Scalp Care at 40%, Licensing and Regulation at 20%, with Nail Care and Skin Care each around 6% ([PSI Texas cosmetology candidate bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). Minnesota's PSI bulletin uses a different category structure, with "Safety and Infection Control" alone at 25% ([PSI Minnesota bulletin](https://proctor2.psionline.com/media/programs/704.pdf)). Study broad from a textbook, then check your specific state bulletin so you know where to spend the last two weeks of review.

## Original practice questions (write these cold, then check yourself)

These are original questions we wrote to mirror the style and category weighting of a typical state board written exam. They are not from any Milady, PSI, or NIC exam.

1. What is the primary purpose of a patch test before a chemical hair service?
   A) To check hair porosity
   B) To detect a potential allergic reaction to the product
   C) To determine processing time
   D) To measure scalp pH
   **Answer: B**

2. Which of the following is an example of a contraindication for a facial treatment?
   A) Dry skin
   B) Active cold sore or open lesion
   C) Combination skin
   D) Mild dehydration
   **Answer: B**

3. A client's hair test shows high porosity. What does that generally mean for chemical processing time?
   A) Processing time should be increased
   B) Processing time should be decreased
   C) Porosity has no effect on processing time
   D) A patch test replaces the need to consider porosity
   **Answer: B**

4. Which disinfection level is required for implements that come into contact with blood or body fluids?
   A) Low-level disinfection
   B) Sanitization only
   C) High-level (hospital-grade) disinfection
   D) Rinsing with water is sufficient
   **Answer: C**

5. What is the main difference between a cosmetologist's and an esthetician's scope of practice in most states?
   A) Estheticians cannot use any chemical products
   B) Estheticians are generally limited to skin and hair-removal services, not hair cutting/coloring or nails
   C) Cosmetologists cannot perform facials
   D) There is no practical difference
   **Answer: B**

6. Which of the following is a licensing board's typical requirement before issuing a license by endorsement from another state?
   A) The applicant must retake the full training program regardless of prior hours
   B) The applicant must hold an active license in good standing and often meet a minimum-hours or exam-equivalency standard
   C) Endorsement is automatic in all states with no requirements
   D) Endorsement only applies to esthetician licenses
   **Answer: B**

7. Why do state boards require fresh disinfectant solution daily rather than reusing a batch?
   A) Fresh solution smells better
   B) Disinfectant efficacy degrades over time and with repeated use, reducing its ability to kill pathogens
   C) It is a cosmetic industry tradition with no functional purpose
   D) Only wet sanitizers require daily replacement, not others
   **Answer: B**

8. A client discloses a known allergy to PPD (paraphenylenediamine). Which service category does this most directly affect?
   A) Manicure services
   B) Permanent hair color services
   C) Facial waxing
   D) Shampooing
   **Answer: B**

Study each wrong answer, not just the right one. If you missed a question because you didn't know a definition, that's a content gap; if you missed it because you misread the question under time pressure, that's a pacing problem, and those need different fixes.

## FAQ

**Is Milady the same as the state board?**
No. Milady is a textbook and study-platform publisher owned by Cengage Learning. State boards (like TDLR) set licensing requirements and contract exam administrators like PSI or NIC to write and grade the actual exam.

**Can I pass my state board exam using only Milady's textbook?**
Milady's textbook covers the underlying content most state exams draw from, but the weighting and specific categories vary by state. Cross-check your state's official candidate bulletin so your last weeks of study match how your exam is actually structured.

**What is CIMA?**
CIMA is Milady's digital learning platform, used by many cosmetology schools for coursework and assessments. CIMA ExamReady is the specific module built around exam preparation.

**Does passing a Milady-branded practice test guarantee I'll pass my state exam?**
No practice test from any publisher can guarantee a pass, since the actual exam is written and scored by your state's exam vendor, not by Milady. Use practice material to find weak categories, then confirm those categories against your official bulletin.

**Are Milady and PSI or NIC the same company?**
No. They're separate organizations. Milady publishes study material; PSI and NIC administer exams for state boards. Milady's own materials state clearly that PSI and NIC were not involved in producing Milady's products.
