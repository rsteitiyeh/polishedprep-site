Cosmetology exam fees vary more than most candidates expect: New York charges $15 for its written exam and $15 for its practical, while Pennsylvania charges $93 for a single written attempt, and Texas splits its written and practical into $55 and $76 separately. None of these figures include your state's separate license application fee, and school tuition, typically $5,000 to $20,000 or more, dwarfs exam costs in every state.

What follows is the actual fee breakdown for the states we verified directly against current candidate bulletins and state fee schedules, not an estimated national average, since averaging fees this different into one number would hide more than it reveals.

## What does the exam itself cost, state by state?

| State | Written exam fee | Practical exam fee | Retake fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | $55 | $76 | Same as initial ($55 / $76), each attempt paid separately |
| Pennsylvania | $93 (flat, includes procedural skills; no separate practical) | N/A (practical not required) | $93, same as initial attempt |
| New York | $15 | $15 | Same fee structure applies to each new exam application |
| Ohio | $40 (Cosmetology/Branch) | Included in the same application process | $35 |

Sources: Texas fees from PSI's candidate bulletin ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)); Pennsylvania from its candidate handbook ([Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)); New York from its Department of State licensing page ([New York Department of State](https://dos.ny.gov/become-cosmetologist)); Ohio from its official fee chart ([Ohio State Cosmetology and Barber Board, Fees](https://cos.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cos/for-licensees/fees)).

## What's the separate license or application fee?

Exam fees and license fees are usually two different line items, billed by two different offices in states that use an outside testing vendor. New York's Department of State charges a $40 initial application fee on top of its $15 written and $15 practical exam fees, though it offers a combined $50 option if you're also applying for a temporary license at the same time ([New York Department of State](https://dos.ny.gov/become-cosmetologist)). Ohio's new personal license fee for Cosmetology or Advanced Cosmetology is $45, separate from its $40 initial exam fee ([Ohio State Cosmetology and Barber Board, Fees](https://cos.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cos/for-licensees/fees)). California folds its exam and initial license into a single combined application fee rather than billing them as two separate charges, so check your specific license type's current fee on the Board's own application forms before budgeting ([California Board of Barbering and Cosmetology](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/applicants/national.shtml)).

## Why do some states cost less than others?

States that dropped the practical exam tend to sit at the lower end of total exam cost, since you're only paying for one test instead of two. California and Pennsylvania both eliminated their practical exam requirement, which removes an entire fee line that Texas and Ohio candidates still pay. That said, a lower exam fee doesn't always mean a lower total cost to license: a state's separate application, background check, or business-license fees can offset savings from a shorter exam.

## What actually costs the most: the exam, or the school that gets you there?

School tuition is the real expense in cosmetology licensing, not the exam fee. Programs typically run $5,000 to $20,000 or more depending on total training hours required and whether the school is public or private, and training hour requirements themselves vary by state, from around 1,000 hours in New York to over 2,000 hours in states with higher requirements. A candidate who's shopping for the cheapest possible exam fee while ignoring a $15,000 gap between two schools in the same state is optimizing the wrong number.

## What if you fail and have to pay again?

Budget for at least one retake fee going in, even if you don't expect to need it. Texas and Pennsylvania both charge the same amount for a retake as for the original attempt, so a failed written exam in Texas costs another $55, and a failed exam in Pennsylvania costs another $93. Ohio's retake fee ($35) is actually cheaper than its initial exam fee ($40). A well-prepared first attempt is the cheapest path through licensing purely on avoided retake fees, before you even count the lost time.

## Is reciprocity cheaper than testing from scratch?

Often, yes, if your state offers it. A licensee moving from a state with an equivalent license can frequently apply through reciprocity or endorsement instead of retesting, paying an application and reciprocity fee instead of full exam fees. Ohio, for example, lists a $70 reciprocity fee for cosmetology license holders relocating from another state ([Ohio State Cosmetology and Barber Board, Fees](https://cos.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cos/for-licensees/fees)), which is less than its combined initial exam and license fees for someone testing from zero. Check your destination state's specific reciprocity or endorsement list before assuming you'll need to retest at all.

## FAQ

**How much does the cosmetology state board exam cost?**
It depends heavily on your state. Texas charges $55 for the written exam and $76 for the practical. New York charges $15 for each. Pennsylvania charges a flat $93 for its written exam and requires no separate practical. None of these include your state's separate license application fee.

**Is the license fee included in the exam fee?**
Usually not; they're billed separately in most states. New York charges a $40 application fee on top of its $30 combined written and practical exam fees. California is an exception, combining exam and initial license costs into a single application fee.

**Does a retake cost the same as the original exam?**
In Texas and Pennsylvania, yes, the retake fee matches the original exam fee exactly. Ohio's retake fee is actually lower than its initial exam fee.

**What's the biggest cost in becoming a licensed cosmetologist: the exam or the school?**
The school, by a wide margin. Exam and license fees typically total under $200 across every state we checked, while cosmetology school tuition runs $5,000 to $20,000 or more depending on your state's required training hours.

**Can I avoid retesting entirely if I move to a new state?**
Sometimes, through reciprocity or endorsement, if your destination state recognizes your current license. Fees for that path are usually lower than a full initial exam and license fee, but availability depends on which two states are involved, so check the destination state's specific reciprocity list.
