NIC and PSI aren't competing versions of the same exam. NIC, the National-Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology, develops and owns standardized exam content and passing standards that many states adopt. PSI Services LLC is a testing company that some of those states hire to actually deliver the exam: running test centers, scheduling appointments, and scoring results. One writes the test, the other administers it, and in some states PSI runs its own separate exam program that isn't NIC's content at all.

## What does NIC actually do?

NIC develops the content outlines, sample questions, and passing standards behind the theory, practical, and written-practical exams used as a shared base in many states. Its own site describes the arrangement directly: NIC "offers national Theory, Practical and Written Practical examinations for cosmetology and related fields that are standardized, valid and legally defensible," while actual test delivery, "Computer Based testing, Practical examinations and Remote testing," is handled by outside test administration companies or by the state itself, "upon approval and authorization of NIC" ([NIC Testing, NIC Exam Types](https://nictesting.org/nic-exam-types/)). NIC is a standards and content organization, not a company you schedule a test appointment with.

## What does PSI actually do?

PSI Services LLC is a private testing company that multiple state boards contract with to deliver licensing exams, cosmetology among many other professions. In Texas, for instance, the state's exam bulletin states plainly: "The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) has contracted with PSI Services LLC (PSI) to deliver its examinations" ([PSI Texas Cosmetology Operator Candidate Information Bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). PSI runs the test centers, schedules appointments, proctors the exam, and issues score reports. Whether the content behind that exam is NIC's standardized outline or something else depends on the state.

## Here's the part most candidates miss: PSI doesn't only deliver NIC's exam

California is the clearest example. The state's board describes its written exam as "the national examination developed by PSI services," not NIC's content, adopted "effective July 1, 2022" after review by the state's Department of Consumer Affairs ([California Board of Barbering and Cosmetology](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/applicants/national.shtml)). So PSI is sometimes a pure delivery vendor for NIC's content, and sometimes the developer of its own separate national exam program, depending on which state you're looking at. Seeing "PSI" on your candidate bulletin doesn't automatically tell you whose content outline you're studying against; you have to check.

## How do other states handle this differently?

Not every state uses PSI at all. Florida and Pennsylvania both contract with Pearson VUE instead, a different testing company entirely, for exam delivery ([Florida DBPR Cosmetology Candidate Information Booklet](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/servop/testing/documents/cos_cib.pdf); [Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)). New York and Ohio skip outside vendors entirely and administer their own exams directly through their state licensing agencies ([New York Department of State](https://dos.ny.gov/become-cosmetologist); [Ohio State Cosmetology and Barber Board](https://cos.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cos/pathway-to-licensure/exams/exams)).

| Entity | What it does | States using it (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| NIC (National-Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology) | Develops standardized exam content, outlines, and passing standards; doesn't run test centers itself | Content basis adopted by many states through their chosen vendor |
| PSI Services LLC | Delivers exams: scheduling, test centers, proctoring, scoring; content is sometimes NIC's, sometimes PSI's own program | Texas (NIC-based content), California (PSI's own national program) |
| Pearson VUE | A separate testing company; delivers exams, unrelated to PSI | Florida, Pennsylvania |
| State board (self-administered) | State develops and runs its own exam with no outside vendor | New York, Ohio |

## So which one should you actually be studying for?

Neither name on its own. The content outline in your specific state's current candidate information bulletin is what you study, and that bulletin is published by whichever entity actually developed the content, sometimes NIC, sometimes the vendor directly, sometimes the state board. Checking whether your state uses PSI or Pearson VUE tells you where to schedule and how exam day will run. It doesn't tell you what's actually on the test until you open that state's own bulletin and read the content outline inside it.

## FAQ

**Is NIC the same company as PSI?**
No. NIC develops standardized cosmetology exam content and passing standards. PSI Services LLC is a separate, private testing company that some states hire to deliver exams, including some (but not all) NIC-based exams.

**If my state uses PSI, am I taking the NIC exam?**
Not necessarily. Texas's PSI-delivered exam is built on content NIC-style outlines resemble, but California's PSI-delivered exam is explicitly described by the state board as "the national examination developed by PSI services," a separate program from NIC's content. Check your own state's candidate bulletin to know which content outline actually applies.

**Do all states use PSI or NIC content at all?**
No. Florida and Pennsylvania use Pearson VUE, a different vendor entirely. New York and Ohio administer their own exams directly with no outside testing company involved.

**Is PassCosmo affiliated with NIC or PSI?**
No. PassCosmo is an independent exam-prep resource. We are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by NIC or PSI Services LLC, and we reference both by name only to explain how licensing exams are actually structured.

**How do I find out which entity's content my state actually tests?**
Pull your state board's current candidate information bulletin. It will name the testing vendor and, in most cases, explicitly state whether the content is based on NIC's standardized outline or a vendor-specific program.
