Cosmetology written exams generally set the bar between 70% and 75%, but that percentage isn't calculated the same way everywhere. Texas scores you on the raw percentage of questions you answered correctly: 70% correct, no adjustment. Florida and Pennsylvania instead report a scaled score, where 75 doesn't mean "75% of questions right" but a converted number calibrated to that exam version's difficulty. Mixing up the two is the most common misunderstanding candidates have about their result.

There's no single passing score set by a national body that every state inherits. Each state board sets its own cut score, usually in coordination with whichever vendor delivers its exam, which is why Texas's 70% and Florida's 75% aren't actually measuring the same thing despite looking close on paper.

## What's the difference between a raw percentage and a scaled score?

A raw percentage is exactly what it sounds like: the share of scored questions you got right. Texas states this directly in its candidate bulletin: "100 Scored Items, 120 Minutes, 70% Correct to Pass" ([PSI Texas Cosmetology Operator Candidate Information Bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). Get 70 of the 100 scored questions right and you pass, regardless of which specific questions you missed.

A scaled score is a converted number, not a straight percentage. Pennsylvania's candidate handbook spells this out about as clearly as a testing vendor ever does: "The scaled score that is reported to you is neither the number of questions you answered correctly nor the percentage of questions you answered correctly," because different versions of the same exam vary slightly in difficulty, and the scaling process adjusts for that so a 75 means the same thing no matter which version you sat ([Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)). Florida uses the same model: "Examination scores are reported as a scaled score. The minimum score needed to pass has been scaled to seventy-five (75%)" ([Florida DBPR Cosmetology Candidate Information Booklet](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/servop/testing/documents/cos_cib.pdf)).

Practically, this means a Florida or Pennsylvania candidate can't back-calculate "I need to get X questions right" the way a Texas candidate can. Both are pass or fail decisions, but only one of them maps directly to a percentage of the specific test you sat.

## Does the practical exam use the same kind of passing score as the written exam?

In states that still require a practical, it's usually scored as raw points rather than a scaled score, since a practical is graded task by task against a checklist instead of pooled test forms of varying difficulty. Texas's practical is worth 119 total points across its timed sections, and the passing threshold is stated as a straight percentage of those points: 84 out of 119, which is 70% ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). That's the same raw-percentage logic as Texas's written exam, just applied to points earned per procedure step instead of questions answered.

## Is there a single "NIC passing score" every state uses?

No, and this is worth being direct about because it's a common assumption. The National-Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology develops standardized exam content, but individual state boards set the passing score, generally in coordination with their testing vendor rather than by inheriting one fixed NIC-wide number. Pennsylvania's own handbook describes its passing score as "set by the Pennsylvania State Board of Cosmetology (in conjunction with Pearson VUE)," language that puts the decision with the state, not with a single shared source ([Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)). Texas's 70% and Florida and Pennsylvania's scaled 75 make that variation concrete: don't assume your state's cut score without checking its own current candidate bulletin.

## Why does this distinction actually matter to how you study?

If your state uses a raw percentage, you can set a concrete study target: missing 30 of 100 questions on a Texas-style exam still passes, so a strong grasp of most content, not flawless recall, clears the bar. If your state uses a scaled score, don't try to reverse-engineer a specific number of questions you can afford to miss, since the scaling adjusts per test form and a rough personal count won't match the reported result. Either way, run a full timed practice test under real conditions before exam day so your actual performance, not a guess about the math behind the cut score, tells you where you stand.

## FAQ

**What's a passing score on the cosmetology state board written exam?**
It's usually between 70% and 75%, but the exact number and how it's calculated depends on your state. Texas requires 70% correct as a raw percentage; Florida and Pennsylvania both require a scaled score of 75, which is not the same as 75% of questions correct.

**What's the difference between a raw percentage and a scaled score?**
A raw percentage is a direct count of correct answers out of total scored questions. A scaled score is a converted number that adjusts for slight difficulty differences between different versions of the same exam, so it doesn't map one-to-one with the percentage of questions you got right.

**Is the practical exam passing score calculated the same way as the written exam?**
In Texas, yes: both the written exam (70% correct) and the practical exam (70%, or 84 of 119 total points) use the same raw-percentage logic. Other states may score practicals differently, so check your state's current candidate bulletin.

**Does NIC set one passing score every state uses?**
No. NIC develops standardized exam content, but each state board sets its own passing score, generally with its testing vendor. Texas's raw 70% and Florida and Pennsylvania's scaled 75 show this isn't uniform across states.

**Can I calculate exactly how many questions I can miss and still pass?**
Only if your state uses a raw percentage, like Texas. If your state reports a scaled score, like Florida or Pennsylvania, the passing threshold doesn't translate directly into a specific number of missable questions, since scaling adjusts per exam version.
