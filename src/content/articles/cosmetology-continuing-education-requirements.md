Most states don't require continuing education to renew a cosmetology license at all. According to a licensing fee and CE tracker that monitors all 51 U.S. jurisdictions, roughly 16 of 51 currently mandate some amount of CE, checked as of February 2026 ([GetLicenseMap](https://getlicensemap.com/blog/cosmetology-license-renewal-requirements)). Where a state does require it, the hour count typically runs 4 to 8 hours per renewal cycle, and the specific topics required, sanitation, state law, human trafficking awareness, vary by board. If you're assuming your state requires CE because a colleague licensed elsewhere mentioned it, check directly. Assuming the opposite is just as risky.

## Which states actually require hours, and how many?

Here's a direct comparison of six states with verified, sourced CE requirements, three with an established rule and three with newer or higher hour counts worth flagging.

| State | Hours per cycle | Breakdown | Effective / source date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | 4 (under 15 years licensed); 2 (15+ years) | 1 hr sanitation, 2 hrs cosmetology content, 1 hr human trafficking (under 15 yrs); 1 hr sanitation + 1 hr human trafficking (15+ yrs) | Effective September 1, 2025 |
| Georgia | 5, waived on your first renewal | 3 hrs health and safety, 2 hrs any board-approved topic | Current, per GA SOS |
| Louisiana | 0 | No CE requirement of any kind | Current, per LSBC |
| Maryland | 6 | State Board-approved courses, all licensees | Effective January 1, 2026 |
| Iowa | 6 | 4 hrs technical instruction, 2 hrs Iowa law and sanitation | Current |
| Nebraska | 8 | At least 4 hrs must be hands-on skill or service instruction | Current |

Sources: TDLR's continuing education page ([tdlr.texas.gov](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/barbering-and-cosmetology/individuals/continuing-education.htm)), the Georgia Secretary of State's nail technician how-to guide ([sos.ga.gov](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-guide-nail-technician-nail-tech-instructor)), the Louisiana State Board of Cosmetology's licensing FAQ ([lsbc.louisiana.gov](http://www.lsbc.louisiana.gov/faq_ind_license_info.aspx)), and GetLicenseMap's state-by-state CE tracker, checked February 2026 ([getlicensemap.com](https://getlicensemap.com/blog/cosmetology-license-renewal-requirements)).

## Why did Texas add a CE requirement in 2025 after having none?

Because TDLR issued a new rule tying CE hours to how long you've held your license, effective September 1, 2025, ending a stretch where Texas cosmetologists renewed with no coursework requirement at all. This is the single most common reason a Texas renewal gets flagged in 2026: the applicant assumes the old CE-free rule still applies. The hour count scales down the longer you've held your license, 4 hours under 15 years, 2 hours at 15 years or more, which is an unusual structure among states that require CE at all; most set one flat hour count regardless of tenure.

## Is Maryland's new requirement a sign more states are adding CE?

It's one clear data point in that direction, not proof of a trend. Maryland's 6-hour requirement takes effect January 1, 2026, meaning a state that previously had no CE mandate now does, the same pattern Texas followed a few months earlier. Two mid-decade additions in one region don't establish a national trend, but they do mean the "most states require none" baseline is worth rechecking periodically rather than treating as permanent, especially if you're licensed in a state that's historically had no requirement.

## Does hour count correlate with anything predictable?

Not closely. Nebraska's 8-hour requirement is the highest among the six states compared here, more than double Georgia's, but neither state's total renewal fee or process complexity tracks proportionally with the hour count. A higher CE requirement doesn't necessarily mean a harder or more expensive renewal overall, since fee schedules, grace periods, and reinstatement tiers are set independently of the CE hour count.

## What do required hours typically have to cover?

In states that mandate CE, approved courses commonly include:

- Updated sanitation and infection control standards
- State-specific law or regulation changes
- A hands-on skill or technique component, required explicitly in states like Nebraska
- Human trafficking awareness, now a specific line item in Texas's requirement alongside the technical hours

Course format also varies. Some states accept fully online coursework; others require in-person or hybrid delivery for at least part of the hour count. Confirm your state's approved-provider list before enrolling, since credit is typically only granted for board-approved courses.

## Does missing your CE deadline block your renewal?

Yes, in every CE-requiring state we checked. If required hours aren't completed before your renewal deadline, the renewal itself won't process, which puts you in the same position as a missed renewal deadline more broadly, not a separate, lesser issue. Track completed hours throughout your renewal cycle rather than compressing them into the final weeks, when course scheduling conflicts can put an otherwise on-time renewal at risk.

## FAQ

**Does every state require continuing education for cosmetology license renewal?**
No. Roughly 16 of 51 U.S. jurisdictions currently mandate it, per a licensing tracker checked February 2026. The majority require the renewal fee only.

**How many hours are typically required in states that mandate it?**
Among the six states compared here, requirements range from 2 hours (Texas, licensees of 15+ years) to 8 hours (Nebraska), with 4 to 6 hours the more common range.

**Which states added or increased CE requirements most recently?**
Texas added its requirement effective September 1, 2025. Maryland's new 6-hour requirement takes effect January 1, 2026. Both represent states moving from no CE requirement to a mandated one within roughly a year of each other.

**Can continuing education be completed entirely online?**
It depends on the state. Some accept fully online coursework for the entire requirement; others, particularly states requiring a hands-on component like Nebraska, mandate in-person or hybrid delivery for part of it.

**What happens if I complete my CE hours after the renewal deadline?**
Your renewal won't process on time in any state that requires CE, since the hours are due before the deadline, not after it. That puts you in the same escalating-penalty position as a missed renewal more broadly.
