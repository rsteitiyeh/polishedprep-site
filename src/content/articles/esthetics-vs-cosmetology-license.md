*By the PassCosmo team. Published July 15, 2026.*

An esthetics license covers skin care and hair removal only. A cosmetology license covers hair cutting and coloring, nail services, and skin care, and in most states it automatically includes everything an esthetics license allows. Esthetics programs also take fewer training hours: Texas requires 750 hours for an esthetician versus 1,000 for a full cosmetology operator license, and California requires 600 hours for esthetics versus 1,000 for cosmetology.

Which one you need depends entirely on what services you plan to perform. If you only want to do facials, waxing, and skin treatments, a full cosmetology license is more time and money than the job requires. If you want to cut hair or do nails at any point, esthetics alone won't cover it.

## What can an esthetician legally do that a cosmetologist can't?

Nothing. This is the part people get backward. A cosmetology license is the broader credential; it doesn't create a separate skill lane an esthetician lacks. Texas's official scope-of-practice page states it directly: a cosmetology operator "can perform all the services allowed by an Esthetician license and more" ([TDLR esthetician scope of practice](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/barbering-and-cosmetology/scope-of-practice/estheticians.htm)). California's licensing statute follows the same structure: a cosmetologist license "allows them to provide the services of a manicurist and esthetician" on top of hair services ([California Board of Barbering and Cosmetology](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/applicants/license_requirements.shtml)).

## What can't an esthetician do that a cosmetologist can?

An esthetics-only license does not cover hair cutting, hair coloring, chemical hair treatments, or nail services in either Texas or California. Estheticians are limited to skin care and hair removal work: facials, chemical peels within their state's allowed depth, waxing, and similar treatments. If a service involves scissors, clippers, hair color, or a nail file, an esthetics license doesn't cover it, full stop.

## How do the training hours actually compare?

| State | Esthetician hours | Cosmetologist hours | Esthetics as % of cosmetology hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | 750 | 1,000 | 75% |
| California | 600 | 1,000 | 60% |

Texas's esthetician hour requirement is confirmed on TDLR's official application page ([TDLR esthetician application](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/barbering-and-cosmetology/individuals/apply-esthetician.htm)). California's numbers come directly from statute: Business and Professions Code sections 7362.5 and 7364, summarized on the state board's licensing requirements page, which also breaks California's cosmetology 1,000 hours into categories: 100 health and safety, 100 disinfection and sanitation, 200 chemical hair, 200 hairstyling, 150 skin care, 50 hair removal, and 100 manicure/pedicure. California's 600-hour esthetics program breaks down as 100 health and safety, 100 disinfection and sanitation, 350 skin care, and 50 hair removal ([California Board of Barbering and Cosmetology](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/applicants/license_requirements.shtml)).

Notice that even within cosmetology's 1,000 hours, skin care and hair removal together make up only 200 of the 1,000 hours in California, less than the dedicated 400 hours an esthetics-only student spends on the same subjects. A cosmetology license gives you the legal scope to do skin work, but an esthetics program gives you more concentrated hours in it.

## Which license should I actually get?

Get the esthetics license if your career plan stops at skin care and hair removal. It's fewer hours, generally a shorter program, and gets you working in that lane faster. Get the full cosmetology license if there's any real chance you'll want to cut, color, or do nails, even a few years down the road, because upgrading from esthetics to cosmetology later usually means going back to school for the difference in hours rather than a quick add-on exam.

A common mistake: assuming you can "add on" hair services to an esthetics license with a short course. In both Texas and California, the services outside your license's scope require completing the additional training hours and passing the relevant exam category, not a supplemental weekend class.

## Does state licensing law treat the two the same way otherwise?

Mostly, yes. Both licenses in Texas and California require passing a written and practical exam through the state's exam vendor (PSI in Texas), both are subject to the same sanitation and infection-control rules, and both renew on a set cycle with fees. Where they diverge is training hours, curriculum content, and scope of practice, not the general licensing mechanics.

## FAQ

**Can an esthetician do facials under a cosmetology license?**
Yes. A cosmetology license's scope includes everything an esthetics license covers, including facials, in both Texas and California and most other states with a similar licensing structure.

**Is an esthetics license faster to get than a full cosmetology license?**
Generally yes, because it requires fewer training hours. Texas requires 750 hours for esthetics versus 1,000 for cosmetology; California requires 600 versus 1,000. Program length in calendar time depends on your school's schedule, but fewer required hours usually means a shorter program.

**Can I upgrade from an esthetics license to a full cosmetology license later?**
Typically yes, but you'll need to complete the additional required hours in hair and nail services and pass the cosmetology exam. It's not an automatic upgrade or a short add-on course in most states.

**Do estheticians and cosmetologists take the same state board exam?**
No. They're separate license categories with separate written and practical exams that test the specific scope of that license.

**Does every state split esthetics and cosmetology into separate licenses?**
Most do, but the exact structure and hour requirements vary by state. Check your specific state board's licensing page before assuming Texas or California's numbers apply where you live.
