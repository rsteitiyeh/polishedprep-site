Your cosmetology exam score report tells you pass or fail immediately in most states, but what else it shows depends entirely on where you tested. Pennsylvania only discloses your numeric scaled score if you failed, on purpose, so passing candidates can't misuse the number. New York never shows a number to anyone, pass or fail. Texas and Florida both give failing candidates a diagnostic breakdown by content area so you know what to restudy.

Knowing what your report is actually supposed to contain matters for one practical reason: if you failed and got a diagnostic breakdown, that breakdown should drive your retake study plan directly, not get skimmed and set aside.

## Do you find out your result the same day?

In every state we checked, yes. Texas displays your score on screen immediately at the end of a computer-based exam and follows up with an emailed score report the same day ([PSI Texas Cosmetology Operator Candidate Information Bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). Florida issues "an official photo-bearing exam result report immediately following completion" of the exam, right at the test center ([Florida DBPR Cosmetology Candidate Information Booklet](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/servop/testing/documents/cos_cib.pdf)). Pennsylvania's Pearson VUE-delivered exam works the same way, with results available at the test center on exam day.

## Do you actually get shown a number, or just pass/fail?

This is where states genuinely diverge, and it's the part candidates are least likely to expect. Pennsylvania's candidate handbook states its policy outright: "To avoid misuse of score information, numeric scores are only reported to failing candidates." A passing candidate in Pennsylvania sees "pass," full stop, no scaled score attached, while a failing candidate sees the actual number so they understand how close they came ([Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)).

New York goes further in the opposite direction: nobody gets a number, pass or fail. "Examination results are reported as either passed or failed; you will not receive a numerical score," for both the written and practical exams, for every candidate regardless of outcome ([New York Department of State](https://dos.ny.gov/become-cosmetologist)). If you're a strong candidate in New York wondering exactly how well you did beyond passing, that information simply isn't part of the report.

Texas takes a third approach: it displays a numeric percentage score to every candidate, since its passing standard is stated as a direct raw percentage ("100 Scored Items, 120 Minutes, 70% Correct to Pass"), so there's no separate policy needed to hide a scaled number from passing candidates the way Pennsylvania does ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)).

| State | Result shown to passing candidates | Result shown to failing candidates |
|---|---|---|
| Texas | Numeric raw percentage score | Numeric score plus diagnostic report by exam type |
| Pennsylvania | Pass only, no number | Numeric scaled score |
| New York | Pass only, no number | Fail only, no number |
| Florida | Photo-bearing result report, immediate | Same, plus eligibility to request a review session |

## What's in the diagnostic breakdown, if your state provides one?

A diagnostic breakdown tells you which content areas you were weakest in, not just that you failed overall. Texas's written score report explicitly includes this for failing candidates: "the emailed score report will include the diagnostic report indicating your strengths and weaknesses by examination type" ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). This is the single most useful thing on a failing score report, since it turns "you failed" into "you were weak specifically in chemical services and infection control," which is directly actionable for a retake study plan.

## Can you actually see which questions you got wrong?

In some states, yes, through a formal review process, not automatically included on your score report itself. Florida allows candidates who fail to request a review session within 21 days, where they can see the specific questions they answered incorrectly and file a written challenge if they believe a question was flawed; challenges are decided by a psychometrician and subject matter experts, typically within about 21 days ([Florida DBPR Cosmetology Candidate Information Booklet](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/servop/testing/documents/cos_cib.pdf)). This is different from, and more detailed than, the diagnostic content-area breakdown Texas provides automatically; Florida's review shows you individual questions, while Texas's report shows you which category those questions came from.

## What should you do with your score report before you retake?

If your report includes a content-area breakdown, structure your retake study time around it directly instead of restudying everything evenly. If your state only tells you pass or fail with no diagnostic detail, like New York, you won't get that guidance from the report itself; a full practice test that mirrors your state's content categories becomes your substitute diagnostic tool. Either way, don't retake on instinct about what felt hard during the exam; felt difficulty and actual missed content don't always line up.

## FAQ

**Do I find out if I passed the cosmetology exam the same day?**
Yes, in every state we checked. Texas, Florida, and Pennsylvania all provide results immediately at the test center or on screen at the end of a computer-based exam.

**Will my score report show me a number, or just pass or fail?**
It depends on your state. Texas shows every candidate a numeric percentage. Pennsylvania only shows a number to candidates who failed, specifically to prevent passing candidates from misusing the score. New York never shows a number to anyone, pass or fail.

**What's a diagnostic report, and do all states provide one?**
A diagnostic report breaks down your performance by content area rather than just giving an overall result. Texas provides this automatically to failing candidates. Not every state does; check your own state's candidate bulletin for its specific score reporting policy.

**Can I see exactly which questions I got wrong?**
In some states. Florida allows failing candidates to request a formal review session within 21 days of the exam and file a written challenge on specific questions. This is separate from, and more detailed than, an automatic diagnostic content-area breakdown.

**Why would a state hide the numeric score from candidates who passed?**
Pennsylvania's candidate handbook states this is intentional, "to avoid misuse of score information." The idea is that a passing score isn't meant to be compared, ranked, or used as a credential beyond the pass/fail outcome itself, so only failing candidates, who need the number to understand how close they came, receive it.
