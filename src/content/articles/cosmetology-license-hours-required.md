*By the PassCosmo team. Published July 15, 2026.*

Cosmetology training hours range from around 1,000 to 1,600 depending on the state, with no single national standard. Texas, California, New York, and Massachusetts require 1,000 hours; Florida requires 1,200; most states in the 1,500 to 1,600 range include Illinois, Georgia, Arizona, Ohio, and Washington. Virginia cut its requirement from 1,500 to 1,000 hours effective December 1, 2025, one of the clearest recent examples of a state actively lowering the bar.

The hour count you need depends entirely on where you're licensing, not on any federal minimum, and states genuinely change these numbers. If you're comparing programs across state lines, always check your specific state board's current page before enrolling, since a school's marketing material can lag behind an actual rule change.

## Is it true some states dropped to 1,000 hours recently?

Yes, but check the specific state before assuming it applies broadly. Virginia is the clearest confirmed case: its 1,500-hour requirement dropped to 1,000 hours effective December 1, 2025 for students who enrolled on or after September 1, 2024, with students already enrolled under the old rule given until August 31, 2026 to finish under the prior standard ([Virginia DPOR](https://www.dpor.virginia.gov/CosmetologyCurriculum)). California made a similar cut back in 2022, from 1,600 to 1,000 hours. But not every state is trending down: a 2024 proposal to cut Arizona from 1,500 to 1,000 hours stalled and never passed, and Georgia has stayed at 1,500 hours despite similar chatter elsewhere.

Watch out for outdated claims circulating online. Oklahoma is a documented example: search summaries have claimed Oklahoma cut its requirement to 1,000 hours effective July 2025, but Oklahoma's own licensing board FAQ, last modified September 29, 2025, still lists 1,500 clock hours (or 50 credit hours) as current ([Oklahoma cosmetology FAQ](https://oklahoma.gov/cosmo/resources/faqs.html)). Always check the state board's own page over a summarized claim, especially on a number this specific.

## Does an apprenticeship reduce the hours I need?

Usually not, and this surprises people. In most states we checked (Pennsylvania, Illinois, Michigan, Washington, Wisconsin, Georgia), the apprenticeship path requires more total hours than school, not fewer, since on-the-job training hours are typically counted at a lower rate than supervised classroom instruction. Apprenticeship is generally about avoiding tuition and getting paid while training, not about finishing faster. Tennessee is closer to neutral, splitting the same 1,500-hour total between classroom and mentored work rather than adding hours. Check your specific state's apprenticeship rule before assuming it's a shortcut.

## What's the range across all 50 states?

Older national surveys put the range around 1,000 hours on the low end (California, Massachusetts, New York under their prior rules) to roughly 2,100 hours in Iowa, per NIC's 2022 State Education and Licensing Guide. That survey is now several years old and states have moved since then, Virginia's 2025 cut being a direct example, so treat any single "national range" figure as a rough historical baseline rather than a current fact. The reliable way to know your number is your own state board's current page, not an aggregated multi-state list.

## Bottom line

Expect somewhere between 1,000 and 1,600 hours depending on your state, with 1,000 and 1,500 being the two most common landing points among states we checked. If two nearby states have very different requirements and you have flexibility on where to train, the hour difference is worth factoring into program cost and timeline, since tuition is usually priced per hour.

## FAQ

**How many hours do I need for a cosmetology license in Texas?**
1,000 hours, reduced from 1,500 hours in 2019. Confirm the current figure directly on TDLR's cosmetologist application page before enrolling in a program.

**Which states require the fewest cosmetology training hours?**
Texas, California, New York, Massachusetts, and now Virginia (as of December 2025) all currently require 1,000 hours, among the lowest confirmed figures we found.

**Which states require the most hours?**
Washington requires 1,600 hours for the school track. Older national data put Iowa around 2,100 hours, though that figure hasn't been independently reconfirmed against a current Iowa source in this review.

**Does apprenticeship training take fewer hours than cosmetology school?**
Usually not. In most states we checked, apprenticeship paths require equal or more total hours than the school track, since on-the-job hours often count at a reduced rate.

**Do high school cosmetology programs count toward my state's hour requirement?**
In several states, yes, hours completed in an approved high school cosmetology-arts program can count toward the total. Confirm with your state board exactly how many hours qualify and whether the program needs prior state approval.
