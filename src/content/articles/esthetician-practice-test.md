An esthetician practice test worth your time mirrors the National-Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology's Esthetics Theory Examination, which is split into two domains: Scientific Concepts, worth 55 percent of the exam, and Skin Care and Services, worth 45 percent. The exam runs 110 total items, 100 of them scored, over 90 minutes, and most states set the passing score somewhere between 70 and 75 percent of the scored items.

That split matters more than most study guides let on. Scientific Concepts is the larger domain by a meaningful margin, covering microbiology, infection control, anatomy, and skin histology, not the hands-on facial procedures people usually picture when they think "esthetics exam." Below, we cover what's actually on each domain and eight original sample questions written in the same format most state exams use.

## What's on the Scientific Concepts domain (55%)?

This domain covers basic microbiology (bacteria, viruses, parasites, fungi), infection control levels and methods, safety procedures including Standard Precautions and blood exposure protocols, Safety Data Sheets and chemical handling, human physiology and anatomy at the cell, tissue, organ, and system level, histology and physiology of the skin specifically, skin disorders and diseases, hair structure and growth cycles, and basic product chemistry including pH. It's a heavier science load than most people expect walking in, and it's worth more of your score than the hands-on procedures section.

## What's on the Skin Care and Services domain (45%)?

This domain covers client consultation and documentation, draping and client protection, skin analysis and Fitzpatrick typing, contraindications, treatment protocol, cleansing, steaming, exfoliation (chemical and mechanical), massage movements, extraction, mask types and their functions, the conclusion of a facial service (moisturizing, sun protection, home care), basic electrical equipment knowledge, makeup principles and application, and other services including body treatments, lash and brow services, and hair removal methods.

## 8 sample esthetician practice questions (with answers explained)

1. **What is the scientific term for the study of the skin?**
Dermatology. It's a foundational vocabulary question, but exams use it as a gateway into more specific histology and physiology questions later in the same section.

2. **A product containing an antiseptic ingredient reaches which level of decontamination?**
Disinfection, not sterilization. Antiseptics are formulated for use on living tissue and reduce microorganisms, but they don't achieve the complete elimination of all microbial life that true sterilization requires.

3. **What is another name for the basal layer of the epidermis?**
Stratum germinativum. It's the deepest epidermal layer, where new skin cells are produced before migrating upward through the other layers.

4. **During the anagen phase of the hair growth cycle, what is happening?**
The hair is actively growing. Anagen is the active growth phase; catagen is the transitional phase where growth stops, and telogen is the resting phase before the hair sheds.

5. **A new esthetics client is scheduling a series of chemical exfoliation treatments. When should the client's consultation and consent form be reviewed and signed?**
At the first treatment, and reviewed again any time the client's skin condition, medications, or treatment plan changes. A one-time signature at intake doesn't cover a service series if something material changes partway through.

6. **What term describes small, visibly dilated blood vessels seen near the surface of the skin?**
Telangiectasia. It's commonly confused with rosacea on exams, but telangiectasia refers specifically to the visible dilated capillaries, while rosacea is the broader inflammatory condition that can include them as one symptom among several.

7. **In addition to softening sebum before extractions, what's another function of a facial steamer?**
Moisturizing the skin's surface. Steam increases hydration and helps prepare the skin for extraction and product penetration, in addition to its softening effect on built-up sebum in the follicle.

8. **A client with active, weeping cystic acne on the treatment area books a facial with extractions. What's the correct response?**
Modify or decline the extraction portion of the service and refer the client to a dermatologist if the condition looks infected or beyond a standard facial's scope. Exams score active, weeping, or infected skin as a contraindication for extraction regardless of what the client requests.

## How should you use these two domains to plan your study time?

Since Scientific Concepts outweighs Skin Care and Services on the exam itself, don't default to spending more time practicing facial procedures just because they feel more "esthetics" than memorizing skin layers and microbiology terms. A candidate who's strong on extraction technique but shaky on infection control levels and skin histology is under-prepared for the larger half of the actual test. Study proportionally, roughly matching your hours to the 55/45 split, and take a full timed practice run in your final week to see whether that balance is actually reflected in your score.

## FAQ

**How many questions are on the esthetician exam?**
110 total items in the NIC national Esthetics Theory Examination, of which 100 are scored and 10 are unscored pretest questions that don't count toward your result.

**What's the passing score for the esthetician exam?**
Most states set it between 70 and 75 percent of the scored items, though the exact cut score is set by your individual state board, not by NIC itself. Check your state's candidate handbook for the precise number.

**Which domain is worth more on the exam, science or skin services?**
Scientific Concepts, at 55 percent versus 45 percent for Skin Care and Services, based on NIC's own Esthetics Theory Examination content outline.

**Is the esthetics exam the same as the cosmetology exam?**
No. Estheticians take a separate NIC theory exam focused on skin care, with different domain weighting than the broader cosmetology exam, which also covers hair and nail services.

**Do all states use the NIC esthetics exam?**
Most do, though some states administer their own state-specific exam instead. Check your state board's candidate information bulletin to confirm which exam you'll actually be taking.
