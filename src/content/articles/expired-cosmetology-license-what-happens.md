An expired cosmetology license means you're no longer legally authorized to perform paid cosmetology services, and continuing to work anyway can trigger fines or disciplinary action separate from the renewal itself. The fix gets more expensive the longer a license sits lapsed: every state we checked structures its penalty in escalating tiers, not a single flat late fee. Stop paid work, confirm your license's exact status with your state board, and act inside the earliest tier you still qualify for.

The specifics vary meaningfully by state, so what follows walks through three verified fee-and-consequence structures side by side, then the general steps to reinstate.

## What actually happens the moment a license lapses?

Legally, you're no longer authorized to practice, even though nothing about your day-to-day work has visibly changed. This is the part licensees most often underestimate: an expired license isn't a paperwork technicality in the eyes of a state board, it's practicing without a valid license. Depending on the state and circumstances, that can mean civil fines, disciplinary action against your license record, and in more serious or repeated cases, misdemeanor charges.

## How does the penalty escalate with time, state by state?

Here's the direct comparison across three states with clearly published tiers.

| State | Under ~18 months / 1 year lapsed | 18 months to 3 years lapsed | Past 3 years lapsed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | $75 renewal (vs. $50 on-time) | $100 renewal | Cannot renew; must meet initial licensing requirements again, including both exams |
| Louisiana | Online renewal with standard late fee | Restore by paying renewal fee plus penalties | Must pass a restoration exam before reinstatement |
| Georgia | 30-day grace period, late fee on top of renewal fee | Lapsed status; $200 reinstatement application fee required | Same reinstatement process; back-dated CE proof required for every missed cycle |

Sources: TDLR's fee schedule ([tdlr.texas.gov](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/barbering-and-cosmetology/feechanges.htm)), the Louisiana State Board of Cosmetology's licensing FAQ ([lsbc.louisiana.gov](http://www.lsbc.louisiana.gov/faq_ind_license_info.aspx)), and the Georgia Secretary of State's nail technician how-to guide ([sos.ga.gov](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-guide-nail-technician-nail-tech-instructor)).

The pattern across all three: the earliest tier is just money, a late fee stacked on the standard renewal fee. The latest tier stops being about money and starts requiring you to re-prove competency, either through a full new application and exam (Texas) or a dedicated restoration exam (Louisiana).

## Why does Georgia's 30-day window matter so much?

Because it's the narrowest grace period among the three states above, and missing it changes the entire process, not just the price. Inside 30 days past the August 31 deadline, a Georgia nail technician can still renew online or by mail with a late fee. One day past that window, the license moves into a lapsed status requiring a full reinstatement application, a $200 fee, and documented continuing education for every cycle missed, not just the most recent one. A 30-day window leaves far less room for a slow mail delivery or a missed email than Texas's 18-month first tier.

## What are the actual steps to reinstate?

1. **Stop performing paid cosmetology services** immediately, to avoid compounding the situation with an unlicensed-practice violation on top of a lapsed renewal.
2. **Contact your state board directly** to confirm your license's exact current status and which specific tier applies based on how long it's been lapsed.
3. **Gather required documentation**, which might include continuing education completion certificates, updated contact information, or forms specific to reinstatement rather than standard renewal.
4. **Pay the late fee and any reinstatement fee**, which are usually separate line items layered on top of the standard renewal fee.
5. **Complete any additional requirement your tier triggers**, such as Louisiana's restoration exam past three years or Texas's full relicensing process past three years.
6. **Confirm your license shows active** in your state's licensee lookup system before resuming paid work.

## Does an expired license follow you permanently once fixed?

No, generally. Once properly reinstated through whichever tier applied, an expired license is a resolvable administrative lapse, not a lasting disciplinary mark, provided you address it directly rather than continuing to work unlicensed while it sits unresolved. The escalating tiers above exist specifically to make delay expensive, which is the incentive structure pushing you toward fixing it early rather than a punishment for the lapse itself.

## Does it affect moving your license to another state?

Potentially, yes. If you're planning to transfer through reciprocity or endorsement, many receiving states require your original license to be active and in good standing, not lapsed, before they'll process an application. Resolving an expired license in your original state is usually a necessary first step before an interstate transfer, so don't let a lapse sit if a move is part of your plan.

## FAQ

**Can I keep working while my reinstatement application is pending?**
Generally no. Most states, including all three compared above, require your license to show as active before you resume paid services, even while a reinstatement application is being processed.

**How much does reinstatement actually cost?**
It depends on your state and how long the license has been lapsed. Georgia's reinstatement fee alone is $200 past the 30-day grace window, on top of the standard renewal fee and any owed continuing education documentation.

**Will I have to retake the exam if my license expired?**
Only past an extended lapse, and the exact trigger varies. Texas requires a full new application and both exams past three years. Louisiana requires a dedicated restoration exam, also past three years. Shorter lapses in both states are resolved through fees, not re-examination.

**What's the narrowest grace period among the states you checked?**
Georgia's, at 30 days past the August 31 deadline. Texas and Louisiana both give meaningfully more runway, up to 18 months and 1 year respectively, before the penalty structure shifts significantly.

**Does an expired license show up as a permanent mark on my professional record?**
Not typically once reinstated. It's treated as a resolvable lapse, not a disciplinary event, as long as you stop unlicensed work and complete the reinstatement steps that apply to your specific state and lapse duration.
