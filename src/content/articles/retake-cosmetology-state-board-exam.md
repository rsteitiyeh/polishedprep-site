If you fail a cosmetology state board exam, you can retake it, and no state we checked caps how many attempts you get. What does vary is how soon: Texas and Pennsylvania both require a 24-hour wait before you can reschedule, written and practical are always retaken (and repaid) separately, and you re-register through the same vendor or state agency that ran your original exam, not a separate retake process.

The details that actually matter when you're staring at a fail result are how soon you can try again, what it costs, and whether your overall eligibility to test at all has an expiration date. Those three answers differ by state, so here's what's confirmed for the states we checked.

## How soon can you retake the exam after failing?

Texas requires a 24-hour wait after a failed attempt before you can schedule again, stated directly in the candidate bulletin: "you must wait 24 hours before scheduling again to allow all systems to update. You cannot retake the examination on the same day as the failed attempt" ([PSI Texas Cosmetology Operator Candidate Information Bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). Pennsylvania uses the same 24-hour rule before a retake can be rescheduled ([Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)). Florida doesn't publish a fixed waiting period in its candidate booklet; instead, a candidate who fails "may be reexamined upon submission of a reexamination application," with reexamination instructions sent along with the original grade report ([Florida DBPR Cosmetology Candidate Information Booklet](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/servop/testing/documents/cos_cib.pdf)).

## Do you retake the written and practical exams together or separately?

Separately, in every state we checked. If you passed the written exam but failed the practical, you don't retake both; you schedule and pay for the practical alone. Texas states this structurally by requiring the written exam to be passed before you're even eligible to schedule the practical, which means a practical retake never touches your already-passed written result ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). The same separation holds in states with two written exams: Florida's Written Theory and Written Clinical exams are scheduled and scored independently, so failing one doesn't require retaking the other.

## Is there a limit on how many times you can retake the exam?

Not on attempt count, but there can be a limit on time. Texas's exam eligibility window is 5 years, and you may test "an unlimited number of times during this period," meaning the real constraint isn't a cap on retakes but the clock on your overall eligibility ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). Pennsylvania's authorization to test is valid for a shorter window; once it lapses, a candidate has to reapply through the state system (PALS) rather than simply schedule another retake. Check your own state's eligibility period so a slow retake schedule doesn't accidentally let your original approval expire.

## What does a retake actually cost?

Every state we checked charges a fee for each attempt, written and practical priced separately where both exist.

| State | Retake fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Texas | $55 (written) / $76 (practical) | Same as initial exam fee, each attempt paid separately |
| Pennsylvania | $93 (written/procedural skills) | Flat fee, same amount for first attempt or any retake |
| Ohio | $35 (Cosmetology/Branch); $35 (Barber) | Separate no-show fee of $50 applies if you miss a scheduled attempt |

Sources: Texas fee figures are from PSI's candidate bulletin ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)); Pennsylvania's from its candidate handbook ([Pennsylvania Cosmetology Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/clients/pa-cosmetology-and-barber/203900.pdf)); Ohio's from its official fee chart ([Ohio State Cosmetology and Barber Board, Fees](https://cos.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cos/for-licensees/fees)).

## How do you actually schedule a retake?

You re-register through whichever entity ran your original exam, not through a separate retake system. If PSI or Pearson VUE delivered your exam, you log back into your existing candidate account with that vendor and book a new appointment once your waiting period has passed. Texas candidates use the same PSI account they used the first time ([PSI Texas Cosmetology CIB](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). Ohio candidates reapply through the state's eLicense Ohio portal, since Ohio administers its own exam rather than using an outside vendor ([Ohio State Cosmetology and Barber Board, Fees](https://cos.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cos/for-licensees/fees)).

## What should you actually change before your retake?

If your score report included a diagnostic breakdown by content area, which most vendor-delivered exams provide to failing candidates, start there instead of restudying everything evenly. A candidate who failed on chemical services and infection control but scored well on nail and skin care wastes retake prep time reviewing sections they've already demonstrated competence in. Retaking without changing your study approach, especially retaking the same day or the next day out of urgency rather than out of genuine improvement, is a common way to fail twice on largely the same gaps.

## FAQ

**Can I retake the cosmetology state board exam if I fail?**
Yes, in every state we checked, with no cap on the number of attempts. What's limited is timing (a required wait, usually 24 hours, before rescheduling) and, in some states, an overall eligibility window that your retakes have to fall within.

**How long do I have to wait before retaking a failed exam?**
Texas and Pennsylvania both require a 24-hour wait before you can reschedule. Florida doesn't publish a fixed waiting period; instead, you submit a reexamination application using instructions sent with your original grade report.

**If I fail the practical but passed the written exam, do I retake both?**
No. Written and practical exams are scored and retaken separately in every state checked. Passing the written exam isn't affected by a failed practical retake.

**Does a retake cost the same as the original exam?**
Usually, yes. Texas and Pennsylvania both charge the same fee for a retake as for the original attempt ($55 written / $76 practical in Texas; a flat $93 in Pennsylvania). Ohio's retake fee ($35) is actually lower than its initial exam fee ($40) for cosmetology and branch licenses.

**Is there a limit on how many times I can retake before I have to start over?**
Not on attempt count directly, but your overall exam eligibility window can expire. Texas gives you 5 years and unlimited attempts within that window; once it lapses, you'd need to requalify rather than simply retake.
