Bring one valid government photo ID, a full disinfected implement kit packed in a labeled bag, and nothing electronic. Most state boards test cosmetology practical skills through PSI or NIC-developed exams that run three to four hours, cover haircutting, chemical services, nail and skin tasks, and score you on sanitation as much as technique. Pack wrong and you can be turned away before the exam starts, no refund.

The exact list varies by state because each board sets its own candidate bulletin, but the shape is nearly identical everywhere: an ID check, a kit inspection, and a series of timed services performed entirely on a mannequin. Texas, administered through PSI, is a useful reference point because TDLR publishes a detailed current bulletin.

## What ID do I need to bring?

One valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID: a driver's license, state ID card, or passport book. The ID has to show your printed name, a photo, a signature or fingerprint, date of birth, and an expiration date. A passport card, which is different from a passport book, is not accepted at most PSI-administered exams. If your only ID is a school-issued card, check your state's bulletin before exam day since acceptance is conditional and not guaranteed everywhere ([PSI Texas cosmetology candidate bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)).

## What goes in the kit, and how does it need to be packed?

Your kit needs two things right on it: a bag labeled "clean," "disinfected," or "pre-sanitized," and individual containers that are legible in English with no coded numbering system. Graders open the bag before you start.

**Items that must be labeled:**

- Kit bag itself (marked pre-sanitized or disinfected)
- Antiseptic lotion or spray
- EPA-registered disinfectant
- Odorless monomer or primer (the bottle must actually say "odorless")
- Nail adhesive
- Moisturizer and protective cream
- First-aid or blood-exposure kit
- Spray bottle
- Trash bags

**Items that do not need labels:**

- Files, buffers, brushes
- Blow dryer, curling iron, clips, combs
- Cotton, cuticle pusher
- Drapes, capes, towels
- Gloves
- Shears, razor with guard
- Mannequin head and stand
- Tint brush and bowl

Aerosol products are prohibited outright at most PSI-administered practical exams, so trade any aerosol hairspray or setting spray for a pump version before packing.

## What's not allowed in the exam room?

| Prohibited | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Phones and other electronics | Automatic removal from the exam in most states |
| Non-religious hats or head coverings | Flagged during ID/kit check |
| Bulky or loose outerwear | Checked for concealed notes or devices |
| Backpacks, purses, wallets | Not permitted in the testing area |
| Food, drink, gum | No exceptions during the timed portion |
| Pens, paper, reference material | The practical exam is skills-only, no notes |
| Open-toe shoes | Closed-toe shoes are required for the standing/service portions |

## What does the practical exam actually test?

Everything is performed on a mannequin, not a live model, in nearly every state's cosmetology practical exam. Texas's current PSI-administered practical runs 3 hours 31 minutes, is worth 119 points, and requires 70% (84 points) to pass ([PSI bulletin 703](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/703)). The sections typically include:

1. Pre-service setup and implement disinfection
2. Monomer-and-polymer nail tip application
3. Blood exposure incident procedure (a mandatory safety demonstration, not optional)
4. Basic facial and massage
5. Soft-wax eyebrow shaping
6. Haircut (minimum one inch removed all over, using both razor and shears)
7. Permanent wave, minimum six rods
8. Blow-dry and thermal curling
9. Mannequin preparation for chemical services
10. Foil highlights
11. Virgin relaxer application
12. Relaxer retouch
13. End-of-exam disinfection and station cleanup

You're graded on the setup and cleanup steps as much as the service itself. Skipping the pre- and post-service disinfection routine costs points even if the haircut or wave is technically clean, because sanitation is graded as its own category, not folded into the technique score.

## What if I show up missing something?

Graders check the kit before the exam starts, and a missing required item (an unlabeled disinfectant, no first-aid kit, an aerosol product) can mean you lose points on the setup section or, for a serious safety-related gap like no blood-exposure supplies, get turned away entirely. Exam fees are non-refundable and forfeited if you don't test within the window PSI sets, so a packing mistake is an expensive one. Build your kit the night before against your state's actual bulletin, not a generic list, since a handful of items (odorless-labeled monomer, specific disinfectant types) are checked closely.

## FAQ

**Do I need to bring my own mannequin head to the cosmetology practical exam?**
Yes. Candidates supply their own mannequin head and stand as part of the required kit. Check your state's bulletin for any restrictions on mannequin hair color or type, since some exams prohibit markings or coloring on the mannequin's hairline.

**Can I use aerosol hairspray during the practical exam?**
No, most PSI-administered practical exams prohibit aerosol products entirely. Pack a pump-spray version of any styling product you'd normally reach for.

**What happens if my ID doesn't match my exam registration name exactly?**
You may be denied entry. The name on your photo ID needs to match your exam registration. If you've had a legal name change, update it with your state board and the exam vendor before exam day, not the morning of.

**Is the cosmetology practical exam done on a real person or a mannequin?**
Nearly all current state board practical exams are performed entirely on a mannequin, not a live model, which is why kit and mannequin-prep items make up a large share of the checklist.

**How long is the cosmetology practical exam?**
It varies by state, but Texas's current PSI-administered practical runs 3 hours 31 minutes. Check your own state's candidate bulletin for the exact timing, since it's one of the details that differs most between states.
